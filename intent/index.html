Most people and companies never write down what they actually want, so they can't tell progress from motion. I help them write it down. Then I build the software that closes the gap between where they are and that written goal.

What it is, in three lines

  • AIO OS (All-in-One OS): one place for your written target, your context and your checks. Tools work toward the target, and you can tell progress from motion.
  • AIO buddy: the front agent. It helps you say what you want, then hands the work to the right tool.
  • The same move at every level. A life has a target, and so does a month, a week and a task. See the loop.

What I care about

The causes I care about are effective altruism and 80,000 Hours, and AI safety. What I build for them is an intent-mapped AI-Life-OS, with harnesses and validators, so a crew of agents can be trusted with real work. Every project is the same move: write the target down first, then let the tool work toward it.

The same move, one level down

  1. Write the target.
  2. Do the work.
  3. Check the gap.
  4. If the gap is a problem of its own, open a new loop with a smaller target.

A life target has a month target. A month target has a week target. A week target has a task. Each level is the same three beats. The loop is Daniel Miessler's: current state, ideal state, checks anyone can run, climb, record each miss.

The harness behind it

ThoughtSignal is the harness I am building in my own time: a Chief-of-Staff-style AI harness with one human-facing agent. The front agent reconciles your intent and dispatches. Workers are role-scoped and run in isolated copies, validators gate every push, and merges happen only on my word. Backpass reads each session's record and proposes changes to the harness, and I approve them. One base map serves every tool.

Credits

The idea that people split into those who write down their ideal state and those who do not, and the parts of a personal AI harness (a harness that remembers you, a written ideal state, a verification loop, intent engineering, a personal API, reusable skills), come from Daniel Miessler's work: TELOS, TheAlgorithm, LifeOS, Intent Engineering, General Hill-Climbing, Fabric and his daemon.
