I'm Ashish Raina. I run a crew of AI coding agents from one chat, and this site, The Chart Room, shows how: how I steer them, how I check their work, and how I keep them doing what I meant.

"Managing a complex AI agentic bot system is sort of like managing a contract with different vendors."

That is how I work. Each agent is a vendor with a brief, a scope and a deliverable. I review the work, I approve what ships, and I write the rules down so the next one starts from them.

Agents as vendors

A vendor does work you cannot watch all day. You do not read every line it writes. You write down what you want, check what comes back, and decide what happens when it goes wrong. I do the same with agents.

Vendor practiceWhat my setup does
Scope and briefEvery task starts as a brief: intent, spec, rules and a definition of done. A worker does one task and nothing outside it.
OnboardingEvery agent reads the same plain-text rules file before it starts.
Statement of workA permission list says who may do what. Only I send, publish, spend or merge (see Safety by design).
Status reportsA worker writes one status line per phase change. I read reports and drafts as pages, not in a terminal.
AcceptanceWork leaves a phase only when its check passes. Validation is evidence, not opinion.
EscalationA worker asks before anything that is mine to decide, and may not close a task while my question is open.
Audit of claimsA claim is written down, then checked. A claim with no passing check is "in trial", not a feature.
OffboardingEach worker gets its own throwaway copy of the code. Nothing it wrote goes out without my word.

Where the analogy is thin: a human vendor has its own standards and can refuse. An agent has only its brief and the rules file, and by default my workers skip permission checks, so the brief and my review carry the control.

Influences

  • ICM by Jake Van Clief: a short map, routing tables, numbered stages with a contract each. It decides what an agent reads, and when.
  • Kun Chen's agent stack: one dispatcher (FirstMate), throwaway worktrees, a gate before push, rule proposals from logs (backpass) and review pages. It decides how agents are run, checked and corrected.
  • Matt Pocock's skills: strongest on intent capture, specs and review. Ideas I want to borrow: a short interview before a spec, and a shared glossary.

No head-to-head test of these approaches exists here, and the token numbers in each source are their own claims.

How this site is laid out: Chart & Cards

The chart is my second brain: a live ring map of the public notes, FirstMate's parts, the skills, the posts and the pages. It runs behind every page. Each page, this one included, is a card on top of it: a panel on the right on a wide screen, a sheet from the bottom on a phone. Minimise or close the card to see the whole map; click any dot for its own card. HUD and Matrix are its two colour sets. The Second Brain shows the chart on its own.
